The Experience
Users open the app to a start screen where they select a difficulty tier: beginner, intermediate, or advanced. Each tier unlocks more complex parts and physics. From there, they enter a 3D assembly workspace where they drag and drop real rocket components from a parts library to build their own rocket from scratch.
Once assembled, they can test aerodynamics before launch, visualizing drag forces, center of pressure, and stability in real time based on their actual geometry.
At launch, the environment is fully interactive. Wind speed and direction are shown visually, along with temperature, air pressure, and altitude. All of these factors feed directly into the physics simulation and affect how the rocket actually flies.

## What makes this different from OpenRocket
I built this because when I got into rocketry, I looked for tools to help me design rockets, and everything I found was built for engineers who already knew what they were doing. 
OpenRocket is powerful, but it looks intimidating and assumes you already understand the physics. 
I wanted to build something for people like me, someone who thinks rockets are cool but doesn't have a background in aerospace. 
The goal is to make it feel like a game, where you're assembling a real rocket from real parts and watching it actually fly, but everything is explained as you go so you're learning without realizing it. 
It's not trying to replace OpenRocket. 
It's trying to reach the people who would never open OpenRocket in the first place.
